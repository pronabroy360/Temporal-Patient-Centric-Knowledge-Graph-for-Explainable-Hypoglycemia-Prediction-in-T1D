Dear Editors,

Please consider “Temporal Event Graphs and Time-Aligned Pooling for Hypoglycemia Prediction in Type 1 Diabetes: An Exploratory Patient-Disjoint Study,” by Pronab Chandra Roy and Lihui Liu, for the Research and Applications category.

The paper examines whether apparent graph-model advantages persist when non-graph comparators receive the same recorded event information and event-to-CGM timing. In 652 development participants from the Loop public release, a graph exceeded one late-fusion sequence implementation, while a later time-aligned pooling control performed better across two seeds/five folds under a two-epoch budget. A separate eight-epoch pair retained the direction with a smaller difference. We report the architectural confounding, development selection, optimization limits and conditional uncertainty explicitly.

The contribution is an auditable comparative finding rather than a new clinical KG, first-in-field claim or deployed warning system. The supplement and project record document preprocessing, corrected metrics, source-mismatch exclusions, full numerical comparisons and the distinction between retrospective occurrence time and actual event availability. No prospective clinical benefit, independent explanation validation or external graph performance is asserted.
