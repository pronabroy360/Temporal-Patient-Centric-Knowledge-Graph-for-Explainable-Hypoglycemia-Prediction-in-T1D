# Focused prior-work update: event instances, temporal graphs, and matched controls

**Date:** 19 October 2026
**Scope:** A focused check of primary papers most directly overlapping the proposal’s combination of timestamped insulin/meal records, patient-level temporal representation, and hypoglycemia prediction. This is a targeted update, not an exhaustive systematic review. Search terms included combinations of *T1D/type 1 diabetes*, *hypoglycemia prediction*, *knowledge/temporal graph*, *insulin/meal event*, *timestamped*, and *sequence*. Publisher/author primary pages and the conference PDF were inspected.

The JMIR 2026 **Digital Twin Applications in Diabetes Management** scoping review ([article](https://doi.org/10.2196/83059)) searched PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Scopus and Web of Science through 23 June 2025 and included 28 empirical studies. It describes a heterogeneous, mostly retrospective or simulation-based field and includes the 2024 patient-centric diabetes PHKG work. It is useful field context, but its search predates the 2026 HSU project and it is not a systematic review of event-relation hypoglycemia models.

## Defensible novelty boundary

The review found substantial overlap with each broad ingredient: patient-specific semantic KGs for hypoglycemia; timestamped meal/insulin inputs for T1D glycemic prediction; graph-attentive recurrent glucose forecasting; and meal-event-indexed hypoglycemia prediction. Therefore, do **not** claim “the first patient-centric/temporal KG for T1D,” “the first graph for hypoglycemia,” “the first use of timestamped meals or insulin,” or “the first explainable event-aware T1D predictor.”

The narrow candidate contribution is an **empirical comparison**, not the graph ontology alone: under a prespecified rolling incident-low endpoint and patient-disjoint evaluation, test whether explicit relations among individual recorded treatment/meal events and CGM history improve prediction over models receiving the same admissible information, timestamps, training budget, and comparable capacity—including a non-graph event sequence, event/modality summaries, generic-edge graph, and endpoint/time-reconstructed controls. It is also necessary to separate value from event-instance resolution, graph connectivity, and typed relation labels. This targeted search did not identify a primary paper reporting that complete matched comparison. That finding is not proof that no such paper exists; any novelty language must remain provisional until a broader database search and full-text screening are documented.

The current Loop graph-vs-sequence result is only one fold and seed. It supports continued testing of this question, but does not yet validate the candidate contribution. Relation-typed routing did not improve over generic routing in the current pilot. Replication, leakage/availability checks, explanation-fidelity tests, and endpoint/time-reconstruction controls remain necessary.
