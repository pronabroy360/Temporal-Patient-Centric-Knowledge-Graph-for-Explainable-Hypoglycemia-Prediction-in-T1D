**Reviewed:** 29 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Focused primary-source check for the project’s novelty boundary. This is not an exhaustive systematic review and does not establish that no other relevant work exists.

## Other relevant graph and event-context precedents

- “A reinforcement learning approach to effective forecasting of pediatric hypoglycemia in diabetes I patients using an extended de Bruijn graph,” *Scientific Reports* (2024), [primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82649-4). It models temporal glucose patterns with a graph and reports forecasting hypoglycemia within 30 minutes. Its graph is a discretized glucose-pattern graph; it is not the same representation as a multimodal event-instance graph. This further rules out claims that graph-based hypoglycemia prediction itself is new.
- “Feature-Based Machine Learning Model for Real-Time Hypoglycemia Prediction,” *Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology* (2021), [primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8258517/). It predicts <70 mg/dL events at 30- and 60-minute horizons and evaluates insulin/carbohydrate context. Event/context inputs and hypoglycemia prediction therefore predate the KG proposal.
- Ying et al., “GNNExplainer: Generating Explanations for Graph Neural Networks,” NeurIPS (2019), [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03894). GNNExplainer identifies a prediction-specific subgraph and node features. Such attribution is a model explanation technique, not by itself evidence that a relation is clinically correct, causally responsible, or useful to clinicians.
- Choi et al., “GARNN: An Interpretable Graph Attentive Recurrent Neural Network for Predicting Blood Glucose Levels via Multivariate Time Series,” arXiv:2402.16230, [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16230). It uses graph-attention/recurrent modeling for multivariate glucose prediction and interprets variable contribution. It is a forecasting model, not a semantic patient KG or a direct matched test of event relations for future hypoglycemia classification.

## Defensible narrowing for this project

The literature supports rejecting novelty claims such as “first knowledge graph for T1D hypoglycemia,” “first temporal graph for hypoglycemia,” “first explainable graph predictor,” or “first multimodal insulin/food glucose model.” A narrower candidate contribution is:

> **A patient-disjoint empirical test of whether explicit timestamped treatment-event-to-CGM relations improve future hypoglycemia classification over a sequence representation given the same observations, availability assumptions, prediction windows, and model capacity, with relation-specific perturbations and explanation diagnostics.**

This wording is a research target, not a result or a verified “first.” It becomes supportable only after matched controls are genuinely equivalent, the outcome is replicated across the prespecified folds/seeds, information availability is defensible, and explanation quality is evaluated beyond model sensitivity. The current Loop analyses are internal development evidence; they do not supply external validation or clinical validity.

## Access and verification limits

- The ESWC paper PDF and MetaboNet-Bench arXiv full text were accessible and reviewed.
- PubMed and the OUP abstract for Sarwar et al. were accessible. The full JAMIA article/supplement was not retrievable in this review. Therefore dataset split unit, leakage controls, exact event channels, baselines, code availability, and explanation evaluation details remain unverified. Obtain and inspect the full article and supplement before finalizing a manuscript-level novelty comparison.
- This focused search does not establish absence of additional papers. Search the final manuscript’s references and citation network, then repeat a reproducible database search close to submission.
